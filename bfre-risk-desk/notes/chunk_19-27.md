Both have a significant positive correlation of 0.45 over the entire period."
- Earnings and dividends act as proxies for value; earnings are highly cyclical and dividends relatively stable, so both gauge relative valuation at different points of the business cycle.
- The earnings yield factor "is based on different substyles using **the latest reported earnings** and captures asset return commonality between companies with **similar earnings-to-price ratios**"; the dividend yield factor "likewise captures commonality between companies with similar dividends-per-share".

r = X_Mkt f_Mkt + sum_{i in Sty} X_Sty,i f_Sty,i + sum_{j in CInd} X_CInd,j f_CInd,j + sum_{k in CCty} X_CCty,k f_CCty,k + u

### Numbers

- "two passes" — the model estimation is performed in two passes.

### Terms
- First-pass / second-pass estimation.
- Factor blocks: **Market (Mkt)**, **Style (Sty)**, **Core Industry (CInd)**, **Core Country (CCty)**; **Extended Country** factors (estimated in the second pass).
- **Multi-Sector Holding** industry — the one industry excluded from Core Industry factors.
- Country classifications: **Developed**, **Advanced Emerging** (= Core Countries); **Secondary Emerging**, **Frontier**, **Watch** markets (collectively **Extended Countries**).
- Screening criteria for the estimation universe: security type, liquidity, market capitalisation.
- Weighting scheme: **square-root of market capitalisation** regression weights.

### Claims / methodological choices
- Estimation universe "is defined to include only representative assets from each market. In particular, illiquid assets which may have stale prices that do not reflect their true market values are excluded, as are 'tiny' companies whose economic relevance is questionable."
- "Estimation universe assets are screened using information on security type, liquidity and market capitalisation. The methodology used to construct the estimation universe is similar to that used by index providers, and so there is a significant amount of overlap between the assets in the BFRE estimation universes and those found in comparable third-party indexes covering the same region."
- **Two-pass choice:** "The first-pass uses the most important assets in a region (or country) to estimate the most important factors. It excludes assets in markets with lower development status, where data quality and coverage is considerably poorer. Instead, these assets are relegated to the second-pass of the model estimation, and therefore have no impact on the factor returns estimated in the first-pass."
- **Weighting choice + justification:** "Assets are weighted in the regression using square-root of market capitalisation. This provides a good compromise between a weighting scheme that places equal weight on all assets, and one that is weighted by market capitalisation. The former can place too much emphasis on smaller assets in the regression and can lead to a poor fit for mega-cap and large-cap securities, many of which feature prominently in portfolios and benchmarks. Likewise, using (only) market capitalisation may lead to better risk forecasts for mega-caps but would come at the expense of poorer forecasts for mid-cap and small-cap securities. More technically, square-root of market capitalisation adjusts for heteroskedasticity based on the observation that higher residual (specific) risk is typically correlated with smaller market capitalisation assets[14]."
- **Footnote 14:** "Other regression weighting schemes exist which explicitly use the reciprocal of each asset's residual (specific) variance, however, in practice these provide similar results to using square-root of market capitalisation weights."
- **Core Industry definition:** "Core Industry factors include all industries except the Multi-Sector Holding industry (if applicable) as these assets are typically nothing but amalgams of other assets that may already be in the estimation universe."
- **Core Country definition:** "Core Countries are those classified as Developed or Advanced Emerging. These countries are considered to be more representative in the region and so are given higher prominence in the model estimation as compared with Secondary Emerging, Frontier and Watch markets, collectively referred to as Extended Countries. The Extended Country factor returns are estimated in the second-pass of the model estimation."
- The first pass estimates Market, Style, Core Industry and Core Country factors.

### Claims / methodological choices
- **Identification problem:** "This specification is not uniquely identified as there are an infinite number of possible solutions. The reason for this lies in the fact that, for each asset, there exist three intercept terms – the market factor, an industry factor, and a country factor. Every asset has unit exposure to these three factors. This can be resolved by adding two linear restrictions on the definition of the factor returns, which reduces the intercept terms from three to one. This identification simply represents a rotation of the factor returns. It does not impact the efficacy of the risk model."
- **Restriction choice:** "The restrictions force the average[15] industry and country returns to be zero" — equation (1.10).
- **Footnote 15:** "The average return is a square-root of market capitalisation weighted return."
- **Interpretation consequence:** "As a result of these restrictions, the industry and country factors are net of the market factor return, which impacts their interpretation. To illustrate, if asset returns across EMEA are mostly positive on a given day and UK assets are also up but by less than the average return over the region, then the UK factor return will be negative."
- **Single-country models:** "In the case of single-country models, such as Japan, there are no country factors since the market factor is defined on a single country. In this instance only one linear restriction is required – that for the industry factors."
- **Second pass:** "Once the first-pass has been executed, a set of residuals is computed for all assets used in the second-pass to estimate the factor returns on all Extended Country factors and Extended Industry factors." — equation (1.11).
